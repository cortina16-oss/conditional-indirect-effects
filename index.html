Models integrating mediation and moderation · dichotomous moderator W

Conditional Indirect Effects Explorer

In an indirect effect model, the effect of X on Y is at least partially transmitted by mediator M. Sometimes, one or more paths in this model are moderated, resulting in a conditional indirect effect model. Holland, Shore, & Cortina (2017) call these Models Integrating Mediation and Moderation (MIMMs). If you really want to understand the math underlying these things, look no further than Edwards and Lambert (2007).

The purpose of this tool is to illustrate and explain some of the different types of conditional indirect effect model. You can manipulate the values in the input matrices below in order to see what happens to the output. The tool fits the regression equations implied by the model that you specify (with X×W or M×W product terms only on the paths you mark as moderated), then reports the indirect effect of X on Y at W = 0 and W = 1 and the difference between them. Examples follow the papers discussed in Holland, Shore & Cortina (2017, ORM).

Just to keep things manageable, the moderators are assumed to be dichotomous. That way, there can be separate correlation matrices at each level of the moderator. If the moderator is coded 0,1, then the weight for the interaction term is just the difference in slopes between W = 1 and W = 0. For a continuous moderator, the weight would be the rate of change in the slope per unit increase in W.

Estimation: ordinary least squares on the pooled sample, built exactly from each group's n, means, SDs and correlations (W coded 0/1, so X×W equals X in group 1 and 0 in group 0). Paths not marked as moderated are constrained equal across groups. Confidence intervals are 95% Monte Carlo intervals (5,000 draws from the sampling distribution of the coefficients; Preacher & Selig, 2012). With raw data, the bias-corrected bootstrap recommended by Holland et al. is the better choice; Monte Carlo intervals are its usual counterpart when only summary data are available. With SD = 1 in both groups, coefficients are in within-group standard-deviation units.
